# AI Engineering Journal — Week 01

| Field | My Notes |
|---|---|
| **Date** | 2 October 2026 |
| **What I learned that was genuinely new** | The clear architectural distinction between Generative AI (pure next-token inference) and an AI Agent (an iterative software control loop connecting models to memory and external tools). |
| **One AI output I initially trusted** | A battery calculation that claimed 480 Ah was enough for a 48-hour 120W emergency load; it seemed like clean math on the surface. |
| **How I verified it** | Checked battery application standards and realized the model ignored Depth of Discharge (DoD) limits and inverter conversion inefficiencies. |
| **What the AI did well** | Synthesizing dense conceptual definitions into clear, intuitive explanations and identifying comparative tradeoffs between digital architectures. |
| **What the AI could not be trusted to decide** | Engineering sizing involving physical safety margins, operational thresholds, and domain-specific regulatory constraints. |
| **One question I still have** | How do industrial agentic frameworks reliably prevent infinite self-referential execution loops when external APIs return ambiguous error states? |
| **What I will do differently next week** | I will write down my boundary conditions and test criteria *before* asking an AI tool for an answer, rather than evaluating the output backward. |
